BiMGA Knowledge Distillation Pipeline

A step-by-step explainer for the multi-objective KD training diagram

1 Input: MBPP Dataset

Query-Code Pairs

Each training example is a (query, code) pair from the MBPP dataset. The query is a natural language description of a programming task; the code is its solution. A batch of B pairs yields B queries and B documents.

2 Dual Encoding

Teacher Encoder Frozen

all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (fine-tuned in Phase 1, then frozen for Phase 2). Its embeddings are precomputed once and stored — no forward pass during training. This yields fixed teacher embeddings Tq and Td.

Why precompute? The teacher never updates, so computing its embeddings once saves a full forward pass per batch. The student trains against a stable target.

Student Encoder Trainable

TinyBERT (4 layers, 12 heads) — a much smaller model. On every batch it runs a live forward pass over queries and documents, producing Sq and Sd. These update via backpropagation.

3 Scoring + Pairing

Score Matrix Computation

Two in-batch similarity matrices are computed via dot products:

Student scores:   Ssc = Sq · SdT    shape (B × B)
Teacher scores:   Tsc = Tq · TdT    shape (B × B)

Each entry Ssc[i, j] is the cosine-like similarity between query i and document j in the student's embedding space. The diagonal holds the positive (correct) pairs; off-diagonal entries are in-batch negatives.

Critical design choice: Each model scores within its own embedding space. The student uses Sq · SdT, not Sq · TdT. This keeps training consistent with evaluation, where no teacher is available. Cross-space alignment is handled separately by the alignment loss.

4 Multi-Objective Loss

The score matrices and raw embeddings feed into three complementary loss terms. Each addresses a different aspect of what the student should learn:

LCE — Contrastive Retrieval

Standard in-batch contrastive loss (cross-entropy).

CE(Ssc / τ, labels)

Input: Student scores only.
Goal: The student should rank the correct document highest for each query. The diagonal of Ssc should dominate each row.

Analogy: "Get the right answer."

LKL — Score Distillation

KL divergence between score distributions.

KL(softmax(Ssc/τ) || softmax(Tsc/τ)) · τ²

Input: Both score matrices.
Goal: The student's full ranking distribution should match the teacher's — not just the top-1, but the relative confidence across all documents.

Analogy: "Rank everything the way the teacher would."

Lalign — Embedding Alignment

L2 distance between student and teacher embeddings.

||Sq - Tq||2 + ||Sd - Td||2

Input: Raw embeddings (bypasses score matrices).
Goal: Pull the student's embedding vectors directly toward the teacher's in vector space.

Analogy: "Think the way the teacher thinks."

Why three losses? LCE gives the student a task-specific signal (retrieval). LKL transfers the teacher's dark knowledge — relative similarities between non-matching pairs that cross-entropy ignores. Lalign forces the embedding spaces to converge structurally, not just in output rankings. Each addresses a layer of the problem the others miss.

5 Total Loss & Backpropagation

Weighted Sum

L = LCE + λd · LKL + λa · Lalign

The combined gradient updates only the student encoder. The teacher remains frozen. The hyperparameters λd and λa control the relative strength of distillation vs. alignment.

↓

Evaluation

Evaluated on held-out data using MRR, Recall@k, and nDCG. At evaluation time, only the student encoder exists — no teacher, no distillation losses. This is why Ssc must use student-encoded documents during training.

6 What Makes BiMGA Different

BiMGA: Bidirectional Margin-Guided Alignment

BiMGA modifies the alignment loss Lalign in two ways that distinguish it from simpler methods like Embed Distill (which only aligns query embeddings with uniform weight):

Embed Distill (Baseline)

Query-only, uniform weight:

Lalign = mean( ||Sq - Tq||2 )

Aligns only queries. Treats all examples equally regardless of difficulty.

BiMGA (Ours)

Bidirectional, margin-weighted:

wi = σ((posi - hardnegi) / τ)
Lalign = mean( wi · (||Sq - Tq|| + ||Sd - Td||) )

Aligns both queries and documents. Weights each example by the teacher's confidence (margin between positive and hardest in-batch negative). Confident examples get stronger alignment pressure.

Why margin weighting? When the teacher is confident about a ranking (large margin), we trust that alignment signal and push harder. When the teacher is unsure (small margin), we ease off — forcing the student to match an uncertain teacher could hurt more than help.
Why bidirectional? Aligning only queries lets the student cheat — it can learn good query representations that work against the teacher's document space but fail when paired with its own documents at test time. Aligning both queries and documents forces the shared backbone to restructure its internal representations, which is why the CKA analysis shows BiMGA's gains are in the middle layers (L2, L3), not just the output.

Talking Points for the Professor

  1. Scoring is self-contained per model. Ssc = Sq · SdT (not Sq · TdT). This matters because at test time the teacher is gone — training must mirror evaluation conditions.
  2. The three losses are complementary, not redundant. LCE is task supervision. LKL transfers dark knowledge (relative ranking confidence across all in-batch negatives). Lalign operates in embedding space directly, forcing structural convergence that score-level losses cannot achieve.
  3. BiMGA's two innovations are independently testable. The ablation BiMGA Uniform isolates the effect of bidirectionality (both query + doc alignment) from the margin weighting. The fact that BiMGA Uniform already outperforms Embed Distill (query-only) confirms that bidirectional alignment is valuable on its own.
  4. Margin weighting is a confidence-gated curriculum. The sigmoid on the teacher's margin acts as soft gating: when the teacher is confident (large gap between positive and hardest negative), alignment pressure is high; when the teacher is uncertain, the student gets slack. This avoids forcing the student to imitate the teacher's mistakes.
  5. The CKA evidence is non-circular. BiMGA directly optimises embedding L2 distance, so high CKA at the output layer would be trivially expected. The surprising finding is that CKA gains peak at middle layers (L2-L3), meaning bidirectional alignment reshapes the internal processing pipeline, not just the final projection.
